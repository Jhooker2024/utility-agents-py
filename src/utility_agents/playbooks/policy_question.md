# Policy question

An employee asks about an HR policy.

1. List the policies, then read the one the question is about.
2. policy_id is that policy. When no policy answers the question, leave policy_id out: never
   name the closest one.
3. draft answers the question from the policy text only, quoting its figures. When the policy
   does not answer it, say so and point to HR. When the question could mean more than one
   thing, such as two policies or a case a policy does not name, answer each likely meaning
   from its policy, or ask which is meant; never answer one meaning as if it were the only
   one. A short question that does not name its subject, such as "Does that still count?" or
   "How much do I get?", could be about any policy: ask which one is meant, and answer from a
   policy only when it is the one the employee clearly means. policy_id is then the policy of the first meaning answered, or left out when none is.
4. kind is reply. It is needs_attention when the question is about the employee's own situation
   that HR must handle in person: harassment, threats or conflict with a colleague, dismissal,
   long illness, pregnancy, an injury, or anything with legal effect. Then the draft only says
   that HR will contact them in confidence.
5. commitments stays empty: the figures and dates you quote are the policy's, not a promise.
